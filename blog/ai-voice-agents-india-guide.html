AI voice agents in India: a practical guide for business teams

What an AI voice agent is, which calls to hand over first, what to check before you buy, and how to run a pilot that proves value.

What a voice agent is, and what it is not

An AI voice agent is software that holds a real phone conversation. It calls or answers, listens to what the other person says, replies in natural speech, and finishes with an outcome your team can use: a date, an amount, a booking, a reason, a yes or a no.

It is not an IVR. Nobody presses 1 for Hindi. It is not a recorded robocall either. The caller can interrupt, ask a question back, correct a number or say “call me later”, and the agent handles it the way a trained caller would.

For Indian businesses the bar is higher than a clean demo. Calls come in Hindi, English and a mix of both. Lines are noisy. People are busy, driving or on a shop floor. A useful agent has to work on those calls, not only in a quiet room.

Calls to start with

The best first use case is a call your team already makes from a script or a checklist, many times a day, where the answer has to land in a system. A few that work well:

  • Reminders: appointments, renewals, EMIs, fees, service due dates.
  • Confirmations: cash on delivery orders, delivery slots, dispatch dates, interview slots.
  • Follow-ups: open purchase orders, pending documents, quotes sent, payment promises.
  • Qualification: new leads, site visit interest, test drives, admissions enquiries.
  • Feedback: post-delivery, post-service and NPS calls.

Avoid starting with the hardest conversation you have. Start with the one that is most repetitive and most painful to staff. That is where the time comes back fastest.

What to check before you buy

Language, the way your callers speak it

Ask for a live call in the languages your callers actually use, including switches mid sentence. Listen for names, numbers and dates. Those are where agents slip.

Confirmation before anything is saved

A good agent reads back what matters and saves it only on a clear yes. Fillers such as “ok” or “hmm” should never count as agreement. Ask the vendor to show a correction being handled on a live call.

Where the outcome goes

A transcript is not an outcome. Check that every call ends in structured fields written to your CRM, ERP, helpdesk or a sheet, with the recording attached.

Compliance and control

Look for do-not-call scrubbing, calling windows you control, recordings you can play back, an audit trail, and data hosted in India if that matters to you.

A way to reach a person

Some callers will want a human. The agent should book a call back with your team and pass on the notes, so nobody starts from zero.

Two ways in

Business teams should be able to create an agent and run a list without code. Developers should get an API and webhooks for the same agents.

How to run a pilot

  1. Pick one call. One team, one script, one list.
  2. Write down today’s baseline. Calls a day, reach rate, time per call, how long until answers reach the system.
  3. Start small. Run a few hundred calls, listen to a sample, and tune the script together.
  4. Scale what works. Once the outcomes match what your best callers capture, widen the list.

What to measure

MetricWhy it matters
Reach rateHow many people you actually spoke to, after retries and call backs.
Completed outcomesCalls that ended with the answer you needed, not only a conversation.
Outcome accuracySpot-check saved values against recordings.
Time to systemHours from call to the answer landing where your team works.
Team hours returnedTime your people now spend on harder calls instead.
